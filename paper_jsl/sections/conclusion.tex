\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

Every implicational \kl{intermediate logic}, and every \kl{intermediate
logic} in the full language, is axiomatizable by formulas \kl{minimal in}
classical logic, and in fact every single axiom can be replaced by such a
formula of \kl{order} at most $4$. We list some questions that remain.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Komori's original question concerns extensions of $\BCK$. By
  Remark~\ref{rem:base}, every \kl{logic} between $\IL$ and $\CL$ is $\BCK$
  extended by formulas \kl{minimal in} $\CL$. Our construction uses
  contraction (the members of $\Prem$ are used twice in
  Lemma~\ref{lem:F-equiv}, and \kl{guards} are shared by several
  \kl{edge formulas}), so it does not apply to \kl{logics} between $\BCK$
  and $\IL$. Is every extension of $\BCK$ contained in $\CL$ axiomatizable
  over $\BCK$ by formulas \kl{minimal in} $\CL$?
\item In the full language, what is the least \kl{order} of a formula
  \kl{minimal in} $\CPC$ axiomatizing a given \kl{intermediate logic}?
  Examples~\ref{ex:order2} and~\ref{ex:order3} show that it can be $2$ or
  $3$.
\item What is the least \kl{size} of a formula \kl{minimal in} $\CL$
  axiomatizing a given \kl{logic}, compared with the size of a given
  axiom?
\item Can an independent axiom system consisting of formulas \kl{minimal
  in} $\CL$ always be chosen \cite[Open Problem~3]{NM21}?
\end{enumerate}
