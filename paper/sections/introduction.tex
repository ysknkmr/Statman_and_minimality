\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The main theorem}

Komori~\cite{Kom05} studied logics through the \emph{substitution
preorder}: $\beta\sub\alpha$ iff $\alpha$ is a substitution instance of
$\beta$. A formula $\alpha$ is \emph{minimal} in a logic $L$ if $\alpha\in L$
and no strictly more general formula belongs to $L$. For example, Peirce's
law $((a\to b)\to a)\to a$ is minimal in classical logic $\CL$.

The translation $\St$ (Definition~\ref{def:S}) is a variant of the
polarized extension-variable translation $\varphi^+$ of
Jeřábek~\cite[Def.~3.6]{Jer16}, with the wrappers ``$\wedge1$'' removed.
It replaces every compound subformula occurrence $u$
of $\alpha$ by a fresh variable $x_u$. It records the meaning of $x_u$ in a
premise $E_u$, a one-way definition whose direction depends on the polarity
of $u$:
\[
\St(\alpha)=E_{u_1}\to\dots\to E_{u_m}\to x_{\text{root}} .
\]
For Peirce's law, with $x,y,w$ standing for the whole formula,
$(a\to b)\to a$ and $a\to b$,
\[
\St\bigl(((a\to b)\to a)\to a\bigr)
=((a\to b)\to w)\to(y\to(w\to a))\to((y\to a)\to x)\to x .
\]
Each premise $E_u$ contains exactly two connectives. So for implicational
$\alpha$, $\St(\alpha)$ has order at most $4$ and is computed in linear
time.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $L$ be a logic containing the identity axiom
$I=p\to p$. If $\alpha$ is minimal in $L$ and $\St(\alpha)\in L$, then
$\St(\alpha)$ is minimal in $L$.
\end{theorem}

The hypothesis $\St(\alpha)\in L$ holds for every $\alpha\in L$ as soon as
$L$ contains linear implicational logic $\mathbf{BCI}$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:many-logics} below). The axiom $I$ cannot be dropped: there
is a logic containing $\mathbf{BC}$, but not $I$, in which the conclusion fails
(Proposition~\ref{prop:needI}).

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Why the theorem holds}

A generalization of $\St(\alpha)$ can only ``forget'' some identifications
made in $\St(\alpha)$. The point is that each such forgetting can be pushed
back to $\alpha$. The proof has two steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{It suffices to consider one-step generalizations}
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:criterion}). A formula in $L$ is minimal iff no
  formula obtained from it by replacing some occurrences of \emph{one}
  subformula by a fresh variable $z$ is in $L$.
\item \emph{Every one-step generalization of $\St(\alpha)$ decodes to one of
  $\alpha$} (Lemma~\ref{lem:key}). Let $\alpha'$ be such a generalization.
  We give a substitution $\theta$ that maps each (possibly damaged)
  definition in $\alpha'$ to an instance $\gamma\to\gamma$ of $I$, and
  maps the conclusion to a one-step generalization $\beta$ of $\alpha$:
  \[
  \theta\alpha'=(\gamma_1\to\gamma_1)\to\dots\to(\gamma_k\to\gamma_k)\to\beta .
  \]
  If $\alpha'\in L$, then $\beta\in L$ by modus ponens. This is impossible,
  because $\alpha$ is minimal.
\end{enumerate}
Only closure under substitution and modus ponens and the axiom $I$ are
used. So the argument works for any
logic. Polarity is needed only to guarantee $\St(\alpha)\in L$. For Peirce's
law, a generalization of $\St$ that separates the two occurrences of $w$ is
decoded in Example~\ref{ex:decode}.

\subsection{What follows}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:many-logics}
Suppose that one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $L$ is an implicational logic containing $\mathbf{BCI}$ (e.g.\
  $\mathbf{BCI}$, $\mathbf{BCK}$, $\IL$, $\LC$, $\CL$);
\item[(ii)] $L$ is a logic in the language $\{\to,\cdot,0,1\}$ containing
  the full Lambek calculus with exchange $\mathbf{FL_e}$;
\item[(iii)] $L$ contains $\mathbf{FL_{ew}}$ ($\mathbf{FL_e}$ with
  weakening), in a language with $\to$ and some of
  $\cdot,\wedge,\vee,0,1,\bot,\top$ (e.g.\ $\IL$ in the full language).
\end{enumerate}
Then $L+\alpha=L+\St(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha$. If moreover $\alpha$ is
minimal in $L$, then $\St(\alpha)$ is minimal in
$L$.
\end{corollary}

Taking $L=\CL$ for minimality and a base logic $L_0\supseteq\mathbf{BCI}$
for the axiomatization, we obtain a normal form:

\begin{corollary}[Order-$4$ normal form]\label{cor:normal-form}
Let $L_0$ be an implicational logic containing $\mathbf{BCI}$ (e.g.\
$\mathbf{BCI}$, $\mathbf{BCK}$, $\IL$). If $L=L_0+\Gamma$ with every
$\alpha\in\Gamma$ minimal in $\CL$, then
$L=L_0+\{\St(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}$, where every $\St(\alpha)$ is
minimal in $\CL$ and of order at most $4$.
\end{corollary}

For the Komori--Kashima problem \cite{Kom05,Kas05}, this has the following
consequences.
\begin{itemize}
\item Nakamura and Matsuda~\cite{NM21} gave a $\CL$-minimal axiom $G'$ of
  Gödel--Dummett logic $\LC$ of order $5$, and asked whether order $4$
  suffices \cite[Open Problem~2]{NM21}. $\St(G')$ is a $\CL$-minimal axiom
  of $\LC$ of order $4$, so the answer is yes. The formula is displayed in
  Corollary~\ref{cor:LC}.
\item The bound $4$ is optimal: classical tautologies of order at most $3$
  are intuitionistic theorems (Proposition~\ref{prop:order3}).
\item Corollary~\ref{cor:normal-form} holds over $\mathbf{BCK}$, so it also
  applies to Komori's original super-$\mathbf{BCK}$ formulation
  \cite[footnote~1]{NM21}. For the question which logics are axiomatizable
  by $\CL$-minimal formulas \cite[Open Problem~1]{NM21}, one may restrict
  attention to order $4$.
\end{itemize}

The base logic $\mathbf{BCI}$ cannot be weakened by dropping $B$, $C$ or $I$
(Propositions~\ref{prop:needI} and~\ref{prop:weak}). The implicational case of the main theorem
and Corollary~\ref{cor:many-logics}(i) are formalized in Lean~4
(Section~\ref{sec:lean}).

\subsection{Related work and outline}

The translation is not new. Extension variables go back to Tseitin
\cite{Tse68}. Statman~\cite{Sta79} used them to reduce intuitionistic
propositional logic to its implicational fragment (see also
Švejdar~\cite{Sve03}). One-way definitions chosen by polarity appear in the
Plaisted--Greenbaum translation \cite{PG86} and in Mints' normal form
\cite{Min90}. Rybakov~\cite{Ryb97} uses extension variables for rules.
Our translation is essentially that of Jeřábek~\cite{Jer16}. His
$\varphi^+$ \cite[Def.~3.6]{Jer16} introduces a fresh variable for every
non-variable subformula occurrence, uses the same polarized one-way
definitions, and wraps each of them as $E_u\wedge1$. $\St$ differs from
$\varphi^+$ only in dropping these wrappers, in currying the product of the
definitions into iterated implications, and in treating constants as
atoms. With the wrappers, $\mathbf{FL_e}+\varphi=\mathbf{FL_e}+\varphi^+$
holds for every $\varphi$ in the full language \cite[Thm.~3.8]{Jer16}, and
Jeřábek uses this to show that the substructural hierarchy of Ciabattoni,
Galatos and Terui \cite{CGT08} collapses to $\mathcal N_3$ over
$\mathbf{FL_e}$. Our contribution is not the translation but the
observation that, without the wrappers, it preserves minimality. To our
knowledge this has not been observed before. The wrappers are needed for
soundness over $\mathbf{FL_e}$ with additive connectives, but they destroy
minimality (Section~\ref{sec:additive}); this is why our results for the
full language require $\mathbf{FL_{ew}}$.
