\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We consider implicational formulas, built from variables by $\to$, which
associates to the right. A \kl{logic} is a set of formulas closed under
substitution and modus ponens; for a \kl{logic} $L$ and a set $\Gamma$ of
formulas, $L\lplus\Gamma$ is the smallest \kl{logic} containing
$L\cup\Gamma$. A formula $\beta$ is \kl{strictly more general} than a formula
$\alpha$ if $\alpha$ is a substitution instance of $\beta$ but $\beta$ is
not a substitution instance of $\alpha$. A formula $\alpha$ is \kl{minimal}
in a \kl{logic} $L$~\cite{Kom05} if $\alpha\in L$ and no formula strictly
more general than $\alpha$ belongs to $L$. For example, Peirce's law
$((a\to b)\to a)\to a$ is \kl{minimal} in classical logic $\CL$, whereas
$(a\to b)\to(a\to b)$ is not, since it is a substitution instance of the
classical tautology $p\to p$, which is strictly more general. The
\kl{order} of a formula is defined by $\Ord(p)=1$ for a variable $p$ and
$\Ord(\alpha\to\beta)=\max\{\Ord(\alpha)+1,\Ord(\beta)\}$.

Komori and Kashima~\cite{Kom05,Kas05} asked which \kl{logics} are
axiomatizable by formulas \kl{minimal} in $\CL$, and in particular whether
every intermediate logic axiomatizable by such formulas is either
intuitionistic logic $\IL$ or $\CL$. Nakamura and Matsuda~\cite{NM21}
answered the latter question in the negative: they gave a formula $G'$ of
\kl{order} $5$ that is \kl{minimal} in $\CL$ and satisfies
$\IL\lplus G'=\LC$, where $\LC$ is Gödel--Dummett logic. They asked whether
there exists such a formula of \kl{order} $4$ \cite[Open Problem~2]{NM21}.

\subsection{Main results}

We define a map $\St$ on formulas. Let $\alpha$ be a formula. Each
occurrence of a subformula of $\alpha$ has a polarity, positive or
negative: the
occurrence of $\alpha$ itself is positive, and in an occurrence of
$\beta\to\gamma$, the occurrence of $\gamma$ has the same polarity and the
occurrence of $\beta$ the opposite polarity. To each occurrence of a
compound subformula of $\alpha$ we assign a new variable, called an
\kl{extension variable}, distinct occurrences receiving distinct variables; to each occurrence of a variable
$p$ we assign $p$ itself. For an occurrence of a compound subformula
$\beta\to\gamma$ with variable $x$, where $y$ and $z$ are the variables of
the occurrences of $\beta$ and $\gamma$ in it, put
\[
E=\begin{cases}(y\to z)\to x,&\text{if the occurrence is positive},\\
x\to(y\to z),&\text{if the occurrence is negative}.\end{cases}
\]
Then $\St(\alpha)=E_1\to\dots\to E_m\to x_0$, where $E_1,\dots,E_m$ are
these formulas for all occurrences of compound subformulas, listed in
post-order (for $\beta\to\gamma$, first those inside $\beta$, then those
inside $\gamma$, then the one for $\beta\to\gamma$ itself), and $x_0$ is
the variable of $\alpha$ itself; if $\alpha$ is a variable, then
$\St(\alpha)=\alpha$. If an occurrence of a compound formula $\gamma$ is the
only positive (resp.\ negative) occurrence of $\gamma$ in $\alpha$, we
write $\evfp{\gamma}$ (resp.\ $\evfn{\gamma}$) for its variable; thus
$x_0=\evfp{\alpha}$. For example, for Peirce's law
$\alpha=((a\to b)\to a)\to a$,
\[
\St(\alpha)=((a\to b)\to\evfp{a\to b})\to(\evfn{(a\to b)\to a}\to\evfp{a\to b}\to a)
\to((\evfn{(a\to b)\to a}\to a)\to\evfp{\alpha})\to\evfp{\alpha} .
\]
Since every $E_i$ has \kl{order} at most $3$, $\St(\alpha)$ has \kl{order}
at most $4$. (The formal definition is given in Section~\ref{sec:translation}.)
Our main theorem is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $L$ be a \kl{logic} containing $\axI=a\to a$. If $\alpha$ is
\kl{minimal} in $L$ and $\St(\alpha)\in L$, then $\St(\alpha)$ is
\kl{minimal} in $L$.
\end{theorem}

Let $\BCI$ be the linear implicational logic, axiomatized by
$\axB=(b\to c)\to(a\to b)\to a\to c$, $\axC=(a\to b\to c)\to b\to a\to c$
and $\axI$. If $L$ contains $\BCI$, then the hypothesis $\St(\alpha)\in L$
follows from $\alpha\in L$, and $L\lplus\alpha=L\lplus\St(\alpha)$ for every
formula $\alpha$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:many-logics}). We obtain the following
two corollaries.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:LC}
Let $G'$ be the formula of Nakamura and Matsuda \cite[Sect.~4]{NM21}:
\[
G'=A_1\to A_2\to\dots\to A_7\to c ,
\]
where
\begin{gather*}
A_1=a\to a',\quad A_2=b'\to b,\quad A_3=c''\to c',\quad A_4=c'''\to c',\\
A_5=(a\to b)\to c'',\quad A_6=(b'\to a')\to c''',\\
A_7=((a'\to b')\to c'')\to((b\to a)\to c''')\to c'\to c ,
\end{gather*}
and put $G'_i=A_i\to\dots\to A_7\to c$ for $2\le i\le7$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SGprime}
\begin{aligned}
\St(G')={}&(\evfn{a\to a'}\to a\to a')\to (\evfn{b'\to b}\to b'\to b)\\
&\to (\evfn{c''\to c'}\to c''\to c')\to (\evfn{c'''\to c'}\to c'''\to c')\\
&\to ((a\to b)\to \evfp{a\to b})\to (\evfn{(a\to b)\to c''}\to \evfp{a\to b}\to c'')\\
&\to ((b'\to a')\to \evfp{b'\to a'})\to (\evfn{(b'\to a')\to c'''}\to \evfp{b'\to a'}\to c''')\\
&\to (\evfn{a'\to b'}\to a'\to b')\to ((\evfn{a'\to b'}\to c'')\to \evfp{(a'\to b')\to c''})\\
&\to (\evfn{b\to a}\to b\to a)\to ((\evfn{b\to a}\to c''')\to \evfp{(b\to a)\to c'''})\\
&\to (\evfn{c'\to c}\to c'\to c)\to (\evfn{((b\to a)\to c''')\to c'\to c}\to \evfp{(b\to a)\to c'''}\to \evfn{c'\to c})\\
&\to (\evfn{A_7}\to \evfp{(a'\to b')\to c''}\to \evfn{((b\to a)\to c''')\to c'\to c})\to ((\evfn{A_7}\to c)\to \evfp{G'_{7}})\\
&\to ((\evfn{(b'\to a')\to c'''}\to \evfp{G'_{7}})\to \evfp{G'_{6}})\to ((\evfn{(a\to b)\to c''}\to \evfp{G'_{6}})\to \evfp{G'_{5}})\\
&\to ((\evfn{c'''\to c'}\to \evfp{G'_{5}})\to \evfp{G'_{4}})\to ((\evfn{c''\to c'}\to \evfp{G'_{4}})\to \evfp{G'_{3}})\\
&\to ((\evfn{b'\to b}\to \evfp{G'_{3}})\to \evfp{G'_{2}})\to ((\evfn{a\to a'}\to \evfp{G'_{2}})\to \evfp{G'})\\
&\to \evfp{G'}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\IL\lplus\St(G')=\LC$, the formula $\St(G')$ is \kl{minimal} in
$\CL$, and $\Ord(\St(G'))=4$. Hence \cite[Open Problem~2]{NM21} has an
affirmative answer.
\end{corollary}

Every compound subformula of $G'$ occurs exactly once in $G'$, so the
notation $\evfp{\gamma}$, $\evfn{\gamma}$ applies to all variables of
$\St(G')$ other than those of $G'$; the formula~\eqref{eq:SGprime} contains
$30$ distinct variables and $67$ occurrences of variables. The number $4$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:LC} cannot
be replaced by $3$, since every formula of $\CL$ of \kl{order} at most $3$
belongs to $\IL$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:order3}).

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:iff}
Let $L_0$ be a \kl{logic} containing $\BCI$, and let $L$ be a \kl{logic}.
Then $L=L_0\lplus\Gamma$ for some set $\Gamma$ of formulas \kl{minimal} in
$\CL$ if and only if $L=L_0\lplus\Gamma'$ for some set $\Gamma'$ of formulas
of \kl{order} at most $4$ \kl{minimal} in $\CL$.
\end{corollary}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:order3} and Corollary~\ref{cor:LC}, the number
$4$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:iff} cannot be replaced by $3$ when $L_0=\IL$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:iff} applies in particular to $L_0=\BCK$, the base
logic of Komori's original formulation of the problem
\cite[footnote~1]{NM21}.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has been formalized in Lean~4, as have the facts
that, for every \kl{logic} $L$ containing $\BCI$, $\St(\alpha)\in L$ for
every $\alpha\in L$ and $L\lplus\alpha=L\lplus\St(\alpha)$ for every
formula $\alpha$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:lean}).

\subsection{Necessity of the hypotheses}

\begin{theorem}[informal]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The hypothesis $\axI\in L$ cannot be removed from
  Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: there exist a \kl{logic} $L$ containing $\axB$
  and $\axC$ but not $\axI$ and a formula $\alpha$ \kl{minimal} in $L$ such that
  $\St(\alpha)\in L$ and $\St(\alpha)$ is not \kl{minimal} in $L$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:needI}).
\item[(b)] Let $\axK=a\to b\to a$. If $L$ is the \kl{logic} axiomatized
  by $\axC$ and $\axK$, or the \kl{logic} axiomatized by $\axB$ and $\axK$,
  then $\axK$ is \kl{minimal} in $L$ but $\St(\axK)\notin L$; in particular,
  $L\lplus\axK\neq L\lplus\St(\axK)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:weak}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Substructural logics}

\begin{theorem}[informal]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The map $\St$ extends to languages containing fusion $\cdot$,
  $\wedge$, $\vee$ and constants, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds for this
  extension (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-fl}).
\item[(b)] Let $L$ be a \kl{logic} containing $\FLe$ in the language
  $\{\to,\cdot,0,1\}$, or a \kl{logic} containing $\FLew$ in the language
  $\{\to,\cdot,\wedge,\vee,0,1\}$; the latter includes intermediate logics.
  Then $L\lplus\alpha=L\lplus\St(\alpha)$ for every formula $\alpha$, and
  if $\alpha$ is \kl{minimal} in $L$, then so is $\St(\alpha)$
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:fl}).
\item[(c)] Over $\FLe$ in the language $\{\to,\cdot,\wedge,\vee,0,1\}$,
  there is a formula $\alpha$ \kl{minimal} in $\FLe$ such that
  $\St(\alpha)\notin\FLe$, and for every compound formula $\varphi$ the
  translation $\varphi^+$ of Jeřábek~\cite[Def.~3.6]{Jer16} is not
  \kl{minimal} in any \kl{logic} containing $\FLe$
  (Remark~\ref{rem:wrapper}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Extension variables go back to Tseitin~\cite{Tse68}. Statman~\cite{Sta79}
used them to reduce intuitionistic propositional logic to its
implicational fragment (see also Švejdar~\cite{Sve03}). Plaisted and
Greenbaum~\cite{PG86} and Mints~\cite{Min90} use, for each
\kl{extension variable}, only one of the two implications between the
variable and the subformula it stands for, chosen according to the
\kl{polarity} of the occurrence. Rybakov~\cite{Ryb97} uses
\kl{extension variables} in the study of admissible rules.

Jeřábek~\cite[Def.~3.6]{Jer16} defines a translation
$\varphi\mapsto\varphi^+$ of the same kind. It introduces a new variable
$p_\psi$ for each occurrence of a subformula $\psi$ of $\varphi$ that is
not a variable, including constants. If $\psi=\psi_0\circ\psi_1$, then
the formula associated with $p_\psi$ is
$(p_{\psi_0}\circ p_{\psi_1})\Rightarrow p_\psi$ or
$p_\psi\Rightarrow(p_{\psi_0}\circ p_{\psi_1})$ according to the
\kl{polarity} of the occurrence, where $p_\chi=\chi$ for a variable $\chi$
and $\chi\Rightarrow\chi'$ abbreviates $(\chi\to\chi')\wedge1$; if $\psi$
is a constant, it is $\psi\Rightarrow p_\psi$ or $p_\psi\Rightarrow\psi$.
Finally,
$\varphi^+$ is the implication from the fusion of these formulas to
$p_\varphi$. He proves $\FLe\lplus\varphi=\FLe\lplus\varphi^+$ for every
formula $\varphi$ \cite[Thm.~3.8]{Jer16}, and uses this to show that the
substructural hierarchy of Ciabattoni, Galatos and Terui~\cite{CGT08}
collapses to the level $\mathcal N_3$ over $\FLe$. In
\cite[Example~3.9]{Jer16} he also notes that, for the formula considered
there, which contains no lattice connectives, $\to$ could be used in place
of $\Rightarrow$. The map $\St$ differs from $\varphi^+$ in that it uses
$\to$ in place of $\Rightarrow$, replaces the fusion by an iterated
implication, and introduces no variables for constants. Jeřábek does not
consider \kl{minimality}, and $\varphi^+$ does not preserve it
(Remark~\ref{rem:wrapper}). To our knowledge, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has
not been observed before.
