\section{Necessity of the Axiom \texorpdfstring{$I$}{I}}\label{sec:remarks}

In this section we show that the axiom $\axI$ cannot be dropped from
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; that $\axB$ and $\axC$ cannot be dropped from
Corollary~\ref{cor:many-logics} is shown in Appendix~\ref{app:weak}. The
results are not used elsewhere in the paper. \AP We write
$\intro*\BC=\lclos{\{\axB,\axC\}}$.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses $\axI$ only to remove the
\kl{antecedents} $\gamma\to\gamma$ of the formula $\theta\alpha'$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:key}.
The next proposition shows that this use is essential, even over
$\BC$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:needI}
Let $M=(\{0,1,2\},\to,\{0\})$ be the matrix with
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\to&0&1&2\\\hline
0&0&1&1\\
1&0&0&0\\
2&0&1&1
\end{array}
\]
and let $L_M$ be the set of formulas that take the value $0$ under every
valuation in $M$. Then $L_M$ is a \kl{logic} containing $\BC$ but not
$\axI$. Moreover, for $\alpha=(p\to q)\to(p\to q)$ the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\alpha$ is \kl{minimal} in $L_M$;
\item[(b)] $\St(\alpha)=(\evfn{p\to q}\to(p\to q))\to((p\to q)\to \evfp{p\to q})
  \to((\evfn{p\to q}\to \evfp{p\to q})\to\evfp{\alpha})\to \evfp{\alpha}$
  belongs to $L_M$;
\item[(c)] $\St(\alpha)$ is not \kl{minimal} in $L_M$, since the formula
  \[
  \alpha'=(\evfn{p\to q}\to z)\to(z\to \evfp{p\to q})\to((\evfn{p\to q}\to \evfp{p\to q})\to \evfp{\alpha})\to \evfp{\alpha} ,
  \]
  which belongs to $\Gen{\St(\alpha)}$ and is obtained by replacing both
  occurrences of $p\to q$ by $z$, belongs to $L_M$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:main} fails for logics not containing $\axI$, even if
$\St(\alpha)\in L$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The set $L_M$ is closed under substitution, and it is closed under modus
ponens because $0\to y=0$ implies $y=0$. A finite check shows that $\axB$ and
$\axC$ take only the value $0$, whereas $2\to2=1$; hence $\axI\notin L_M$.

(a) We have $\alpha\in L_M$, since the values of implications lie in
$\{0,1\}$ and $0\to0=1\to1=0$. The elements of $\Gen{\alpha}$ are
\[
(z\to q)\to(p\to q),\quad (p\to z)\to(p\to q),\quad (p\to q)\to(z\to q),\quad
(p\to q)\to(p\to z),
\]
\[
z\to(p\to q),\quad (p\to q)\to z,\quad z\to z,\quad z .
\]
Each of them takes a value different from $0$ under some valuation in $M$:
for $z\to z$ take $z=2$, for $z$ take $z=1$, and for each of the remaining
six formulas a refuting valuation is found by a finite check. Hence
$\alpha$ is \kl{minimal} in $L_M$ by Proposition~\ref{cor:criterion}.

(b) and (c) are verified by finite checks as well. In fact, $\alpha'$ is the
type of the linear $\lambda$-term $\lambda fgh.\,h(\lambda y.\,g(fy))$, and
$\St(\alpha)$ is its instance under $z\mapsto p\to q$; both are theorems of
$\BC$.
\end{proof}

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:key} to $\alpha'$ gives $\beta=z\to z$, and the
\kl{antecedent} of $\theta\alpha'$ that would have to be removed is exactly
$z\to z$, which does not belong to $L_M$.
