\begin{abstract}
A formula is minimal in a logic $L$ if it belongs to $L$ and no strictly
more general formula, with respect to the substitution preorder, belongs to
$L$. Komori and Kashima asked whether every intermediate logic
axiomatizable by formulas minimal in classical logic is either
intuitionistic or classical logic, and Nakamura and Matsuda gave a
counterexample of order $5$. We give an explicit axiom of Gödel--Dummett
logic of order $4$ that is minimal in classical logic, which answers their
Open Problem~2; order $4$ is the least possible. More generally, over the
linear implicational logic $\BCI$, a logic is axiomatizable by formulas
minimal in classical logic if and only if it is axiomatizable by such
formulas of order at most $4$. Both results are obtained from a polarized
extension-variable translation $\St$ that preserves minimality: if
$\alpha$ is minimal in a logic $L$ that contains the axiom $a\to a$ and
the formula $\St(\alpha)$, then $\St(\alpha)$ is also minimal in $L$.
If $L$ contains $\BCI$, then $\St(\alpha)\in L$ whenever $\alpha\in L$, and
$L\lplus \alpha=L\lplus \St(\alpha)$. We also show that the results extend to
extensions of $\FLe$ in the multiplicative language and of $\FLew$ in the
full language. The main theorem and the soundness of $\St$ over $\BCI$
have been formalized in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}
